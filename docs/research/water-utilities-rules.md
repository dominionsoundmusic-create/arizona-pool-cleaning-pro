## 1. City of Yuma water

1. City of Yuma drinking water is supplied by the Colorado River and treated at the Main Street and Agua Viva Water Treatment Facilities before delivery to customers.
   Source: City of Yuma, Water Quality Report (annual Consumer Confidence Report; latest listed covers 2025 data, archives back to 2019), https://www.yumaaz.gov/government/utilities/lab-reports/water-quality-report (verified via search summary). Also City of Yuma, Our Water, https://www.yumaaz.gov/government/utilities/utilities-treatment-division/our-water (verified via search summary).
2. Per the 2025 report, Main Street is a conventional surface water treatment plant; the Agua Viva Water Treatment Facility treats both surface water and groundwater. The report carries PWSID AZ0414024.
   Source: City of Yuma, COY Water Quality Report 2025 (PDF, hosted by MCAS Yuma), https://www.mcasyuma.marines.mil/Portals/152/COY%20%20Water%20Quality%20Report%20%202025.pdf (verified via search summary).
3. City of Yuma water is considered "hard," with hardness between 18 and 22 grains per gallon (310 to 380 mg/L). The FAQ says hardness is caused primarily by calcium and magnesium ions that occur naturally in all water, and gives the 18 to 22 grains range as the setting for a water softener.
   Source: City of Yuma, Water FAQs, https://www.yumaaz.gov/government/utilities/utilities-treatment-division/water-faqs (verified via search summary).
4. The same FAQ says many different minerals make up the total dissolved solids (TDS) in the water and that sodium is only a small part of it (it gives sodium per 12 oz serving, not a TDS figure in mg/L).
   Source: City of Yuma, Water FAQs (as above) (verified via search summary).
5. A 2004 ADEQ source water assessment covered the Yuma Main Canal, the "A" Main Canal and groundwater wells used by the city and found low risk of contamination.
   Source: City of Yuma, COY Water Quality Report 2025 (as above) (verified via search summary).
6. City of Yuma conservation page: about 70% of residential water use is associated with turf and landscape irrigation; the city partners with Water - Use It Wisely.
   Source: City of Yuma, Water Conservation, https://www.yumaaz.gov/government/utilities/water-conservation (verified via search summary).

## 2. Nearby towns: water providers

7. Fortuna Foothills area: the former Far West Water & Sewer utilities (about 25,000 connections in the Foothills area of Yuma) were bought by NW Natural Water, which closed the purchase in October 2022; the utilities now do business as Foothills Utilities.
   Source: NW Natural Water press release, "NW Natural Water Closes Acquisition of Far West's Water and Wastewater Utilities" (2022), https://s23.q4cdn.com/611156738/files/doc_news/NW-Natural-Water-Closes-Acquisition-of-Far-Wests-Water-and-Wastewater-Utilities-2022.pdf ; also Business Wire, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20221005005813/en (verified via search summary).
8. City of Somerton runs its own water utility: its Water Division handles raw water supply, treatment and distribution, and the city posts annual Consumer Confidence Reports (2019 to 2024 listed) on its Water Quality Report page.
   Source: City of Somerton, City Of Somerton Water, https://www.somertonaz.gov/departments/public_work_departments/water.php ; City of Somerton, Water Quality Report, https://www.somertonaz.gov/departments/public_work_departments/water_quality_report.php (verified via search summary).
9. City of San Luis water is groundwater pumped from ten wells at six sites in the city, 250 to 600 feet deep, from the Yuma Basin/sub-basin (Colorado River watershed); each site has disinfection and manganese removal equipment. PWSID AZ0414005. The 2024 report lists arsenic at 1.6 ppb (MCL 10 ppb).
   Source: City of San Luis, 2024 Water Quality Report (CCR, testing performed in 2024), https://sanluisaz.gov/Documents/Departments/Public%20Works/Water/CCR-2024%20Digital(u)_202506271349182414.pdf ; City of San Luis, Water Quality Report page, https://www.sanluisaz.gov/departments/public_works/water.php (verified via search summary).
10. City of San Luis posted a notice that third-quarter 2026 testing showed the system exceeded the Total Trihalomethanes (TTHM) standard (MCL 0.080 mg/L, locational running annual average). (Background only; not needed on a pool site.)
    Source: City of San Luis, "Important Information for Our Water Customers regarding Total Trihalomethanes (TTHM)", https://www.sanluisaz.gov/articles/Important-Information-for-Our-Water-Customers-regarding-Total-Trihalomethanes-TTHM-443.php (verified via search summary).
11. Town of Wellton runs its own water utility (Water Department, utility clerk 928-785-3348). Its 2018 CCR lists the source as Colorado River water supplied by the Wellton-Mohawk Irrigation & Drainage District canal system. Newer CCRs (2024, 2025; PWS AZ0414022) are posted.
    Source: Town of Wellton, Town of Wellton 2018 Consumer Confidence Report, https://www.welltonaz.gov/DocumentCenter/View/397/Town-of-Wellton-2018-Consumer-Confidence-Report-PDF ; Town of Wellton, Water Department, https://www.welltonaz.gov/1213/Water-Department (verified via search summary).

## 3. Electricity and gas

12. APS (Arizona Public Service) serves about 1.4 million homes and businesses in 11 of Arizona's 15 counties; its service territory map labels Yuma, Somerton, San Luis and Wellton, and APS lists no-fee payment locations in Yuma, Somerton, San Luis and Wellton.
    Source: APS, Service Area Map, https://www.aps.com/en/Residential/Service-Plans/Service-Area-Maps ; APS in-person payment locations (PDF) (verified via search summary).
13. In Wellton, electric service is provided by the Wellton-Mohawk Irrigation and Drainage District (30570 Wellton Mohawk Drive, 928-785-3351, wmidd.org).
    Source: Town of Wellton, Utilities Services, https://www.welltonaz.gov/1243/Utilities-Services (verified via search summary).
14. APS current residential time-of-use plans (for example Time-of-Use 4pm-7pm Weekdays, rate TOU-E): on-peak is 4 p.m. to 7 p.m. Monday through Friday, year-round; weekends and 12 holidays are off-peak; winter bills (November through April) have a super off-peak period 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. weekdays. Older frozen plans (Saver Choice Plus) keep a 3 p.m. to 8 p.m. weekday on-peak window.
    Source: APS, Rate Schedule TOU-E (PDF), https://www.aps.com/-/media/APS/APSCOM-PDFs/Utility/Regulatory-and-Legal/Regulatory-Plan-Details-Tariffs/Residential/Service-Plans/Time-of-Use-4pm-7pmWeekdays_R-TOU-E.pdf ; APS, Changes are being made to APS time-of-use plans, https://www.aps.com/en/Utility/Regulatory-and-Legal/Rate-case/Rate-Case-TOU-Transition (verified via search summary).
15. APS pool pump advice: on a time-of-use plan, set the pool pump to run in off-peak hours (on the 4pm-7pm plan, before 4 p.m. on weekdays); APS says a customer could save up to $120 a year by running the pool pump during winter super off-peak hours; ENERGY STAR in-ground pool pumps use 18% less energy than standard versions; turn off the pool heater in summer.
    Source: APS, Energy Saving Tips, https://www.aps.com/en/Residential/Save-Money-and-Energy/Your-Energy-Your-Options/Energy-Saving-Tips ; APS, Time-of-Use 4pm-7pm Weekdays, https://www.aps.com/en/Residential/Service-Plans/Compare-Service-Plans/Time-of-Use-4pm-7pm-Weekdays (verified via search summary).
16. Southwest Gas lists Yuma in its Southern Arizona region.
    Source: Southwest Gas, Arizona Bill Assistance / contact pages, https://www.swgas.com/en/arizona-bill-assistance (verified via search summary).
17. Southwest Gas: if you see, hear or smell signs of a gas leak, leave the area immediately, then call 911 and Southwest Gas at 877-860-6020, whether you are a customer or not. Do not smoke, use flames or switches, or start or stop engines; tell others to leave.
    Source: Southwest Gas, Home & Business Safety, https://www.swgas.com/en/safety ; Southwest Gas, Contact Us, https://www.swgas.com/en/contact-us (verified via search summary).

## 4. Draining pools and backwash water

18. City of Yuma: whenever possible, drain a pool or spa into the sanitary sewer system, not the storm drain. Do not add chlorine for 48 hours before discharging. Run the hose into the cap of the private property sewer cleanout; do NOT use public manholes or cleanouts. Discharge in low-use sewer hours (afternoons or late at night) with a small pump with flow control so nothing spills. If there is no cleanout, the fallback is the lawn, taking care the water does not run off into the street. Utilities Department: 373-4500 (928 area code).
    Source: City of Yuma Engineering, Only Rain Down the Drain (Stormwater Management Program), https://www.yumaaz.gov/government/engineering/stormwater-management-program (verified via search summary).
19. City of Yuma sewer use code lists swimming pool drainage among discharges not allowed into the sewer unless specifically authorized by the Director of Utilities. (Writers: tell readers to call City of Yuma Utilities before draining.)
    Source: Yuma City Code, § 191-02 General Sewer Use Requirements, https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/yuma/latest/yuma_az/0-0-0-53232 (verified via search summary).
20. Yuma County (unincorporated areas such as Fortuna Foothills): the county pool and spa drainage guide allows discharge to landscaping, the sanitary sewer, or (under conditions such as no runoff onto neighboring property and no erosion) a paved road with curb, storm drain or wash. Chlorine must be at or below 1 part per million (1 mg/L) before release; water going anywhere but the sewer must be clear, colorless and free of solids, floating material and debris; pool water may not be permanently connected to a storm drain, channel or wash; saltwater pools may drain only to the sanitary sewer. It references the ADEQ AZPDES De Minimis General Permit.
    Source: Yuma County, pool/spa discharge guide (PDF), https://www.yumacountyaz.gov/home/showdocument?id=24283 (verified via search summary; the search tool noted the PDF text came through garbled in places, so keep wording general).
21. Yuma County has a stormwater ordinance (Regulating Stormwater Quality Management and the Discharge of Stormwater, Sept. 5, 2007); Environmental Programs handles stormwater pollution complaints in unincorporated areas.
    Source: Yuma County, Ordinances, https://www.yumacountyaz.gov/government/county-administrator/ordinances ; Yuma County, Environmental Programs, https://www.yumacountyaz.gov/government/development-services/divisions/environmental-programs (verified via search summary).

## 5. Pool barriers / fences

22. City of Yuma zoning code: all swimming pools must be enclosed by a fence or wall at least five feet high measured from the exterior; openings no larger than four inches; no climbable features; gates self-closing and self-latching. Pools sit at least five feet from side, rear and street-side property lines.
    Source: Yuma City Code, § 154-15.05 Swimming Pools, https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/yuma/latest/yuma_az/0-0-0-46864 (verified via search summary).
23. City of Yuma has adopted the 2018 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code (City Code § 150-018); the city also adopted 2024 IBC, IRC, IEBC and IPMC, effective November 3, 2025.
    Source: Yuma City Code, International Swimming Pool and Spa Code, https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/yuma/latest/yuma_az/0-0-0-52864 ; City of Yuma, Local Building Code Amendments, https://www.yumaaz.gov/government/community-development/building-safety/local-building-code-amendments (verified via search summary).
24. City of Yuma drowning-prevention release: gate latch 54 inches above the walking surface, self-latching and lockable; where the house opens directly to the pool, doors need listed pool alarms; Building Safety inspects new pools for barriers and alarms; power-operated covers are effective and any cover should be secured after every swim.
    Source: City of Yuma, "Barriers, Alarms, and Supervision Among Keys to Drowning Prevention", https://www.yumaaz.gov/Home/Components/News/News/2178/308 (verified via search summary).
25. Yuma County Building Safety handles pool permits as their own permit type; Development Services phone (928) 817-5000; the county publishes a "Swimming Pool Enclosure Requirements" brochure (contents not seen).
    Source: Yuma County, Building Safety, https://www.yumacountyaz.gov/government/development-services/divisions/building-safety ; Yuma County, Informational Brochures on Zoning Questions, https://www.yumacountyaz.gov/government/development-services/divisions/planning-zoning/informational-brochures-on-zoning-questions ; Yuma County, FAQ, https://www.yumacountyaz.gov/government/advanced-components/faq (verified via search summary).
26. A.R.S. 36-1681 (state pool enclosure law): barrier at least five feet high on the exterior side, fully enclosing the pool area; no opening that a 4-inch sphere can pass; at least 20 inches from the water's edge; no outside footholds or handholds; gates self-closing and self-latching, opening outward from the pool, latch at least 54 inches above the ground (or a pool-side release at least 5 inches below the top of the gate, or a padlock/key/electric/combination lock). Public and semipublic pools are excluded. Political subdivisions may have their own pool enclosure ordinances (the summary described these as needing to be at least as strict). Violation is a petty offense; no fine if a compliant barrier is installed within 45 days and an approved pool safety course is completed.
    Source: Arizona Legislature, A.R.S. 36-1681 Pool enclosures; requirements; exceptions; enforcement, https://www.azleg.gov/ars/36/01681.htm (verified via search summary).

## 6. Green pools, mosquitoes, West Nile

27. Yuma County Public Health Services District says green, unmaintained pools are one of the major breeding sources of West Nile virus mosquitoes. Residents can report large stagnant water and green pools through the Service Request feature at yumacountyaz.gov or by calling Yuma County Vector Control at (928) 317-4584.
    Source: Yuma County, "County Health District asks community to help FIGHT the BITE", https://www.yumacountyaz.gov/Home/Components/News/News/2148 ; Yuma County, "West Nile season in FULL SWING, please take precautions", https://www.yumacountyaz.gov/Home/Components/News/News/515 (verified via search summary).
28. West Nile virus is present in Yuma County: the county has announced positive West Nile mosquito pools (one near Mittry Lake) and a locally acquired human West Nile case. County releases say the local West Nile season typically runs June to October.
    Source: Yuma County, "FIGHT THE BITE: Positive West Nile Mosquitoes Found in Yuma County", https://www.yumacountyaz.gov/Home/Components/News/News/402 ; Yuma County, "First locally-acquired case of West Nile Virus detected in Yuma County", https://www.yumacountyaz.gov/Home/Components/News/News/4627 (verified via search summary; release dates not shown, so do not attach a year).
