Dominion Digital Group operates Yuma Pool Cleaning Pro under a d/b/a. The name covers two things: a free phone number, and this website. It is not a pool service, and it never has a crew in anyone's backyard.

What the line does

When you dial the number, a recorded assistant answers. It is there every hour of every day. It asks what is happening with your pool and which part of the Yuma area you are in, then transfers you to a Yuma-area pool business with local owners and no tie to the line. While the transfer connects, that business is played a brief recap of what you said, which saves you repeating it. When no one there is free, the assistant collects who you are, a number to reach you, your part of town and what the pool is doing, and forwards all of it for a return call.

Everything after that is between you and the pool business. Someone from that business inspects, names a figure, carries out the job once you say yes, and collects payment from you. The line has no say in any of those steps.

What it does not do

The line will

  • Answer your call at any hour, free of charge
  • Pass you to a local pool business, along with a summary of the problem
  • Send your details on for a callback if nobody picks up
  • Publish guides with their sources listed

The line will not

  • Clean, repair, inspect or quote any pool
  • Book appointments or set prices
  • Vouch for a company's license, insurance or reviews
  • Charge the person who calls

Why it exists

Pool trouble comes in many forms. One week it is cloudy water; later it might be a screeching motor, a cold heater or a pale crust creeping along the tile. Searching for the right kind of business for each problem takes time that a July pool does not give you. The idea behind the line is a single dial-in covering all of those troubles, for homes in Yuma, Fortuna Foothills, Somerton, San Luis, Wellton and Gadsden.

It picks up at 2 a.m. on a Sunday as readily as at noon on a Tuesday. The call goes to a pool business based locally, not to a national call center. And because no one should hire a contractor blind, the site shows you how to check a company yourself on the Arizona Registrar of Contractors website.

Checking a company before you hire it

The line does not look up licenses or vouch for anyone. You can do it in a few minutes. Ask the business for its Registrar of Contractors (ROC) number, then type it into the free ROC contractor search. The result shows whether the license is current, what kind of work its class allows, recent closed complaints and bond details. Coverage for accidents is another matter. According to the ROC FAQ, workers' compensation for businesses with employees is the only insurance it makes a condition of licensing, and insurance does not appear in the search results. Request a certificate from the business itself if that matters to you.

What the guides try to do

The pages on this site aim to be useful even if you never call. Each one is written for Yuma conditions: Colorado River water that the City of Yuma itself describes as hard, long stretches of triple-digit heat, monsoon dust, and homes that sit empty for part of the year. Every number on a page comes from a named public source, and each page lists those sources at the bottom so you can check them.

On prices, the guides are careful. No published source lists pool service prices for Yuma specifically, so the cost guide quotes national ranges and names who published them. A real quote depends on your pool, its condition and the company. Where a job involves acid, gas or electricity, the guides say to leave it to a professional and explain why.

How the line is paid

Calling costs you nothing, now or later. Pool businesses receiving calls may send Yuma Pool Cleaning Pro a referral fee. That is how the line earns money, and a note saying so sits in the footer on all pages of the site. The price of the pool work itself is set only by the company doing it.

What changed on this site

An earlier version of this website had a contact form and made broad statements about Yuma's climate and about pool service that could not be backed up. Both are gone. The site now works by phone only, and its pages were rewritten from scratch with checked facts. If you read something here that seems out of date, the sources list on that page is the place to confirm it.
