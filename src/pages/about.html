Yuma Pool Cleaning Pro is a d/b/a of Dominion Digital Group. It is two things: a free phone number, and this website. It is not a pool service, and it never has a crew in anyone's backyard.

What the line does

When you dial the number, a recorded assistant answers. It is there every hour of every day. It asks what is happening with your pool and which part of the Yuma area you are in, then transfers you to a pool business that is independent and locally owned. Before that business picks up, it hears a short summary, so you do not have to start over. If nobody can answer, your name, callback number, area and the pool problem are sent on so the business can phone you back.

Everything after that is between you and the pool business. It comes to look at the pool, quotes its own price, does the job if you agree, and you pay it directly. The line has no say in any of those steps.

What it does not do

The line will

  • Answer your call at any hour, free of charge
  • Pass you to a local pool business, along with a summary of the problem
  • Send your details on for a callback if nobody picks up
  • Publish guides with their sources listed

The line will not

  • Clean, repair, inspect or quote any pool
  • Book appointments or set prices
  • Vouch for a company's license, insurance or reviews
  • Charge the person who calls

Why it exists

Pool trouble comes in many forms. One week it is a cloudy pool, the next a pump that screeches, a heater that will not light or a white crust creeping along the tile. Searching for the right kind of business for each problem takes time that a July pool does not give you. The idea behind the line is simple: one number for every kind of pool problem, across Yuma, Fortuna Foothills, Somerton, San Luis, Wellton and Gadsden.

It picks up at 2 a.m. on a Sunday as readily as at noon on a Tuesday. The call goes to a pool business based locally, not to a national call center. And because no one should hire a contractor blind, the site shows you how to check a company yourself on the Arizona Registrar of Contractors website.

Checking a company before you hire it

The line does not look up licenses or vouch for anyone. You can do it in a few minutes. Ask the business for its Registrar of Contractors (ROC) number, then type it into the free ROC contractor search. The result shows whether the license is current, what kind of work its class allows, recent closed complaints and bond details. Insurance is a separate question: the ROC FAQ explains that, apart from workers' compensation for businesses with employees, it does not require licensees to carry insurance, so ask the company for proof directly.

What the guides try to do

The pages on this site aim to be useful even if you never call. Each one is written for Yuma conditions: Colorado River water that the City of Yuma itself describes as hard, long stretches of triple-digit heat, monsoon dust, and homes that sit empty for part of the year. Every number on a page comes from a named public source, and each page lists those sources at the bottom so you can check them.

On prices, the guides are careful. No published source lists pool service prices for Yuma specifically, so the cost guide quotes national ranges and names who published them. A real quote depends on your pool, its condition and the company. Where a job involves acid, gas or electricity, the guides say to leave it to a professional and explain why.

How the line is paid

Calling costs you nothing, now or later. The pool companies that take calls may pay Yuma Pool Cleaning Pro a fee for each referral. That is the whole business model, and it is stated in the footer of every page. The price of the pool work itself is set only by the company doing it.

What changed on this site

An earlier version of this website had a contact form and made broad statements about Yuma's climate and about pool service that could not be backed up. Both are gone. The site now works by phone only, and its pages were rewritten from scratch with checked facts. If you read something here that seems out of date, the sources list on that page is the place to confirm it.
