How often do you service a pool in Yuma?

How often you service a pool comes down to how much it is used, how much blows into it and how much you do between visits. In Yuma, where heat and sun work on the water most of the year, a weekly visit is what most owners settle on.

How often should you service your pool in summer?

At least weekly. The National Weather Service puts Yuma's average first 100 degree day on April 25 and the last on October 6, with about 118 such days in a normal year. Heat in the water and intense sunlight use up chlorine quickly, while the monsoon season (the National Weather Service dates it from mid-June through the end of September) brings gusts and dust. A NOAA weather memo on blowing dust says the low desert logs the most dust reports in the state, and that those reports climb sharply in July. After a storm, an extra cleaning can make sense.

How often to service a pool in the cooler months?

Some owners switch to a visit every other week in the mild winter months, when the pool sees less use. Angi's national guide matches every-other-week service to moderate use and monthly service to light use, and notes that fewer visits leave more time for buildup, so plan to empty baskets and test the water yourself between visits. Agree on any change of schedule with the company in writing.

How often is pool service needed if I am away for months?

A pool still needs care when nobody is home. Yuma County says its population grows by about 90,000 in winter, and many of those seasonal homes have pools that sit unused while their owners are away. A pool left alone in summer can go green fast if a pump fails or the chlorine runs out. Keep weekly visits going, or at the very least have someone check the equipment every week. The Yuma pool maintenance schedule has a plan for the months away.

About the free line

What happens when I call?

A recorded assistant picks up, any day and any hour. It wants to know the trouble with the pool and your part of the Yuma area, then connects you to a pool business with local owners. If that business cannot pick up, your details and the problem are forwarded for a return call. See how the line works.

Will the line send someone to my house?

No. The line has nobody to send. The business you are connected with decides when to visit, what to charge and how to do the job, and it bills you itself.

Which places does it cover?

Yuma, Fortuna Foothills, Somerton, San Luis, Wellton and Gadsden. The service areas page covers each one.

Costs and quotes

Why do pool service quotes differ so much?

Pool size, trees and debris, a spa, a salt system, the condition of the equipment and whether chemicals are billed on top all change the number. National guides do not agree with one another either, which is why the pool maintenance cost guide names each publisher next to its range.

What should a written quote spell out?

The visit day and how often, what each visit covers, whether chemicals are part of the price or extra, how repairs are priced and approved, and how either side can end the arrangement. A clear list protects both you and the company.

Pool care in Yuma's climate

Why does a Yuma pool lose so much water?

Dry air, heat and sun. The EPA's WaterSense program says an uncovered pool can lose 12,000 to 31,000 gallons a year to evaporation, depending on where it is, and that a cover can stop up to 95% of that loss. A simple way to separate evaporation from a leak is the bucket test. Fill a bucket with pool water, stand it on a step inside the pool, mark the level inside the bucket and the pool level outside it, then look again a day later. If the pool drops much more than the bucket, call about a leak.

Why does white crust form on the tile?

The City of Yuma rates its Colorado River tap water as hard, at 18 to 22 grains per gallon. Evaporation takes only water; calcium and magnesium remain in the pool, and refilling brings in a fresh dose. Layer by layer, that mineral load hardens at the waterline. See tile cleaning and calcium scale.

When should the pump run?

If you have APS on a time-of-use plan, its on-peak hours on current plans are 4 to 7 p.m. on weekdays. APS advises running the pool pump outside those hours, and says running it in the winter super off-peak window, 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. on weekdays from November to April, could save up to $120 a year. Check your own plan, since older plans use a 3 to 8 p.m. window.

Repairs and licensing

What does an R-6 license cover?

The Registrar of Contractors describes R-6 as service and minor repair of home pools. It leaves out gas piping, wiring past the first disconnect and hookups to drinking water lines. Building a pool or fully replacing a plaster or pebble finish calls for a pool contractor class such as B-5. Read more on the pool repair page.

Should I replace a pump that still works?

Not because of the federal rule. Under Department of Energy standards, new pool pump motors in the common size range (1.15 up to 5 total horsepower) built on or after September 29, 2025 must offer variable speed, but nobody has to remove a pump that still runs. See pool pump repair.

Safety

How do I keep children safe around a backyard pool?

For kids aged 1 through 4, the CDC says no cause of death is more common than drowning. Never leave a child alone near water, even briefly. Inside Yuma city limits, city code requires a fence or wall at least 5 feet high around a pool, with gates that shut and latch on their own; elsewhere, ask Yuma County or your city about local barrier rules.

Is it safe to combine two pool chemicals in one bucket?

No. According to the CDC, combining pool chemicals, for example a chlorine product and acid, may give off poisonous gas. Store each one in its original labeled container, dry, shaded and secured away from children and pets, and follow the label.

What if I smell gas near the pool heater?

Get away from the equipment immediately and do not touch switches or light anything. From somewhere safely away, dial 911, then Southwest Gas on 877-860-6020. Gas work is only for a qualified professional.

A neighbor's pool has gone green. Can I report it?

Yuma County Public Health says green, neglected pools are a major source of West Nile virus mosquitoes. You can report one through the service request page on the county website or by calling Yuma County Vector Control at (928) 317-4584.
