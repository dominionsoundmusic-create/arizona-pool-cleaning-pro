How much is pool maintenance? There is no single answer, because the price depends on the pool, the yard, how often someone comes and what the bill includes. This guide gathers the ranges that national cost publishers have printed, explains what tends to push a Yuma quote higher or lower, and gives you a short list of questions so you can compare quotes on equal terms.

Please read one thing first. Yuma Pool Cleaning Pro does no pool work and has no prices of its own; it is a free phone number. When you call, your call is passed to an independently owned pool business near Yuma, and that business decides its price once it knows your pool. Every number on this page comes from the publisher named beside it, and each one is linked in the sources at the end.

Published ranges: how much is pool maintenance and cleaning

Cost publishers collect what homeowners and contractors report, so each one lands on a slightly different figure. That is normal. Use the table to get a feel for the scale of the cost, not to predict a bill. We looked for a range published for Yuma or for Arizona and did not find one, so every row below is national.

Common repair costs, for scale

Repairs are priced apart from routine service. A service visit is when many problems get noticed, but the fix itself is quoted as its own job. These national figures show the size of the most common repair bills. The linked pages on this site explain each repair in more detail.

Before you approve a repair, ask which Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC) license the company holds, and its number. The ROC's R-6 classification is the one meant for servicing home pools and making minor repairs. Type the number into the ROC contractor search (new tab) to see whether it is current. What moves a Yuma quote up or down

A company pricing your pool is really estimating two things: how many minutes each visit takes, and how much product the water will use. Anything that changes either one changes the quote.

Gallons and shape

A bigger pool holds more water to treat and more floor to vacuum. Steps, benches and tight corners add brushing time.

A spa on the side

An attached spa is a second, smaller body of water. It needs its own testing and cleaning on each visit.

Trees and screens

Palms, mesquite and palo verde drop leaves and pods into the water. A screened pool usually stays cleaner, though the screen may need care too.

Salt or chlorine

A salt system makes its own chlorine, so less is bought by the jug. The salt cell still has to be checked and cleaned, and that work may be billed.

How products are billed

A low monthly number with products billed separately can end up costing more than a higher number that includes them. Compare the yearly total.

The busy summer

Pools need the most attention in the hottest months. Ask whether the visit schedule or the price changes from summer to winter.

Months with nobody home

A pool that sat untouched while its owners were away often needs a paid cleanup before it can go on a regular route.

Equipment age

An aging pump or a clogged filter makes every visit longer and makes a repair call more likely.

Starting condition

Clear, balanced water can go straight onto a schedule. Cloudy or green water usually means a separate first job.

Why Yuma conditions add to the work

Most national cost guides do not account for a low desert summer. On average, Yuma sees about 118 days a year when the high reaches 100 F or more (National Weather Service, 1991 to 2020 averages). Sunlight is one of the factors the CDC says reduce chlorine in a pool, and Yuma gets a lot of sun, so a pool here can go through sanitizer faster than a cost guide assumes.

The water itself plays a part. The City of Yuma describes its Colorado River tap water as hard, about 18 to 22 grains per gallon. Every time evaporation lowers the pool and new water goes in, more minerals stay behind, and white scale builds at the tile line. Removing heavy scale is a separate job; the pool tile cleaning page covers it.

Then there is the dust. By the National Weather Service definition, monsoon season covers June 15 through September 30, and one dust storm can fill the skimmer baskets and load up a filter quickly. Ask a company whether a cleanup visit after a storm costs extra.

The months away matter too. According to Yuma County, sun-seeking winter visitors add roughly 90,000 people to the county each winter. If you leave for the summer, the pool sits through the hottest stretch of the year. Decide before you leave who will look after it, and ask what a company charges for those months, because a pool that turns green while you are gone needs a cleanup when you return. For a month-by-month view, see the Yuma pool maintenance schedule.

The power bill for the pump

The service bill is not the only cost. The pump runs every day, and in much of the Yuma area the electricity comes from Arizona Public Service (APS); Wellton is served by the Wellton-Mohawk Irrigation and Drainage District, and in the Foothills or Gadsden your electric bill names your provider. On current APS time-of-use plans, on-peak hours are 4 to 7 p.m. on weekdays, and APS suggests setting the pool pump to run outside those hours. On winter bills, November through April, APS has a super off-peak window from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. on weekdays, and it says running the pool pump then could save a customer up to $120 a year. Check your own plan, because hours differ between plans.

How to get and compare pool service quotes

The Arizona Registrar of Contractors suggests getting estimates from at least three licensed contractors, checking each license, and making sure the price in the estimate matches the price in the contract. That advice is written for contractors in general, and it works well for pool repair. For routine service, the goal is the same: get every quote in writing and make sure each one describes the same work.

Before you call, note your pool's rough size, whether it has a spa, whether it is salt or chlorine, and anything that seems wrong. That way each company quotes the same pool.

Use this checklist with every quote. If a quote leaves an item out, ask about it.

  • Written scope. Which tasks happen on every visit: skimming, brushing, vacuuming, emptying baskets, testing and balancing the water.
  • Visit frequency. Weekly or every other week, and whether that changes between summer and winter.
  • Chemicals. Which products are in the price, which are billed as used, and whether each visit leaves a note of what went in.
  • Filter cleaning. The number of filter cleanings a year, and whether those cost extra.
  • Extra charges. What triggers one: a dust storm cleanup, a green pool, or extra shock.
  • Repairs. How repairs are quoted and approved, and that nothing is replaced without your written okay.
  • License number. The ROC number for any repair work, checked on the ROC contractor search.
  • Insurance. Liability coverage is not listed on the ROC search, so ask the company to show proof.
  • Ending the plan. Contract length, notice to cancel, and whether service can pause while you are away.

Then turn every quote into a yearly total, including the chemicals, before you compare. If you are still deciding whether to hire anyone at all, read is a pool service worth it. To see what a regular visit usually includes, see weekly pool service.

If you keep any pool products at home, follow the CDC: keep each one in its original labeled container, dry and out of the sun, secured away from children and pets, and never mix pool chemicals with each other.

Using the free number to reach a pool company

There is no charge for the call. You speak with an automated assistant first. It notes what seems wrong with your pool and connects you with a nearby pool business that has been given a short summary first. You settle the price, the visit times and the payment with that business.
