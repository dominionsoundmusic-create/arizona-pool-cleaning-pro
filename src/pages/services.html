Yuma pool services fall into a few plain groups: keeping a pool clean week to week, fixing parts that break, and rescuing water or surfaces that have gone wrong. This page explains each one, how they differ, and where to read more, so you know what to ask for before you pick up the phone.

Yuma Pool Cleaning Pro has no pool crew, no vehicles and no chemicals on hand. Its whole job is a free number. An automated assistant picks up around the clock, gathers a short description of the trouble and where you live, then patches you through to a Yuma-area pool business with local owners. From there, that business visits, names a price, carries out the job and sends you the bill. Every service below starts with that same call.

Several signs at once are common. A pump that stopped in July can leave the water green within days, so the pump repair and the cleanup end up as one visit. Tell the assistant everything you see and the company can plan for it.

Routine care: weekly service and cleaning

Weekly pool service is an ongoing arrangement. A pool company comes on a set day, tests and balances the water, cleans what needs cleaning and keeps an eye on the equipment. What you are really buying is the routine: someone notices a slow drop in chlorine or a filter getting clogged before it becomes a green pool or a burned-out motor. The weekly service page covers what a plan usually includes and what to settle in writing before you agree to one.

Pool cleaning is the hands-on work itself: skimming the surface, brushing walls and steps, vacuuming the floor, emptying the skimmer and pump baskets, and cleaning the filter. It can be part of a weekly plan or a single job, such as a deep clean before guests arrive or after a dust storm. The pool cleaning page explains how often each task matters in Yuma.

Both lean heavily on water testing. For home pools, the CDC asks for pH held between 7.0 and 7.8 and a free chlorine reading no lower than 1 ppm (parts per million). If the water contains cyanuric acid, a stabilizer added so sunlight burns off less chlorine, that floor rises to 2 ppm. In a city that the National Weather Service records at about 118 days a year of 100 degrees or more, sun and warm water use chlorine up quickly.

Fixing things: repairs, pumps and equipment

Pool repair is the broad group: leaks, cracks, lights, plumbing, fittings and surface damage. A good repair visit begins with finding the cause, because a water level that keeps dropping could be evaporation, a leaking fitting at the pad or a crack under the plaster, and each one costs very differently. See pool repair in Yuma.

Pool pump repair gets its own page because the pump is the part that works hardest. It moves the water through the filter every day, and when it fails in summer the pool can turn in a short time. The page covers seals, bearings, motors and when replacing makes more sense than repairing. See pool pump repair.

Pool equipment repair covers everything else on the pad: sand, cartridge and DE filters, gas heaters and heat pumps, salt chlorine generators and their cells, valves, timers and automation. See pool equipment repair.

Arizona's Registrar of Contractors (ROC) licenses this kind of work. Its R-6 classification, Residential Swimming Pool Service and Repair, covers servicing home pools and making minor repairs, and the ROC lists what it leaves out: gas piping, wiring past the first disconnect, and hookups to household drinking water lines. Ask any company which license it holds before repair work begins. Electrical and gas work is never a homeowner job: if a reset breaker or GFCI pops again, leave it off and wait for a professional. If you smell gas near the heater, walk away from the equipment at once, avoid switches and flames, and from a safe spot phone 911 and then Southwest Gas on 877-860-6020.

Problem water and surfaces: green pools and calcium scale

Green pool cleanup is for water that has already gone wrong: algae, murky water, or a pool left alone for weeks. It is a bigger job than a normal visit, usually spread over several days of clearing debris, filtering and rebalancing. It is also a safety matter. When you cannot see the bottom, you cannot see a child in trouble, so keep everyone out until it is clear. Yuma County's public health district calls green, neglected pools one of the major breeding sources of West Nile mosquitoes. See green pool cleanup.

Tile cleaning and calcium scale removal deals with the white or gray crust along the waterline. The City of Yuma describes its tap water as hard, at 18 to 22 grains per gallon. Sun and dry air pull water out of the pool, but the dissolved minerals cannot leave with it, so every top-off adds more, and they settle where the water meets the tile. Removing heavy scale without scratching the tile takes the right method, which is why it is its own service. See pool tile cleaning and calcium scale.
