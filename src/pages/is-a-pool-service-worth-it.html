Is a pool service worth it? For some Yuma households, yes; for others, doing the work at home makes more sense. The answer depends less on the pool than on you: how much time you have each week, whether you are here all year, and how comfortable you are handling pool chemicals. This page walks through each part of that decision.

It does not repeat price tables. For published national prices, see how much is pool maintenance. As a single reference point, HomeGuide's 2026 guide puts weekly or every other week service at $80 to $150 a month across the country. Yuma quotes vary from pool to pool.

What doing it yourself takes each week

The Pool and Hot Tub Alliance (PHTA), an industry association, describes a routine most pool owners will recognize. Every week:

  • Test chlorine, pH and alkalinity with a kit or strips, then adjust what is off.
  • Brush the walls and floor so algae and stains cannot settle in.
  • Vacuum dirt off the bottom.
  • Skim the surface and empty the skimmer and pump baskets.

Every month or so, PHTA adds cleaning or backwashing the filter as needed, shocking the water, and looking over the pump, filter and heater for wear. Every few months, it suggests testing total dissolved solids (everything dissolved in the water) and cleaning the tile.

None of this is hard to learn. The real question is whether it will get done every single week, including the weeks you are tired, busy or traveling. The CDC's home pool guidance gives targets to aim for: pH between 7.0 and 7.8, and free chlorine of at least 1 part per million, or at least 2 if the pool uses cyanuric acid, the stabilizer that shields chlorine from the sun. Our Yuma pool maintenance schedule lays the tasks out by season.

Why a Yuma summer is less forgiving

The desert makes skipped weeks more costly. National Weather Service averages for 1991 to 2020 show about 118 days a year in Yuma reaching 100 F or more, and the CDC names sunlight as one of the things that use up chlorine. In a long run of hot, bright days, chlorine can drop faster than a weekend-only routine expects. Once it runs low, algae has room to grow, and clear water can turn cloudy and then green.

Dust is the other summer problem. A NOAA technical memo names southwestern Arizona, Yuma included, as a dust storm area and puts the peak in late July and early August. After a storm, the baskets and filter need attention soon, whether or not it was your week to do it. Evaporation also keeps lowering the water level: the EPA says an uncovered pool can lose 12,000 to 31,000 gallons a year that way, depending on where it is.

A quick way to decide

Read both lists. Whichever one sounds more like your household is probably your answer.

Doing it yourself suits you if

  • You actually enjoy the routine, or at least do not mind it.
  • You live in Yuma all year and are home most weeks.
  • The pool is small and has few trees overhead.
  • You can store chemicals safely, away from children and pets.
  • You are willing to test the water more often during heat waves and after dust storms.

A service may be worth it if

  • You leave for months at a time, or travel often in summer.
  • Weekly work keeps slipping because of a job, family or health.
  • You would rather not keep pool chemicals at home.
  • Your equipment is getting older and you want someone watching it.
  • The pool has turned green before and you do not want a repeat.

The months away

Yuma has a large seasonal population. A 2017 to 2018 study for Visit Yuma estimated about 71,000 winter visitors who stayed 30 days or longer during a December to May window. When seasonal residents head home in late spring, pools at their Yuma houses can sit through the hottest months with nobody there.

An empty house does not stop a pool from needing care. Without regular testing, the water can go green over the summer. That matters to the neighbors too: Yuma County Public Health says green, unmaintained pools are one of the major breeding sources for West Nile virus mosquitoes, and residents can report one to Yuma County Vector Control at (928) 317-4584.

If you are a seasonal resident, the choice is not really between hiring help and doing it yourself, because you cannot do it from far away. The question is who will look after the pool while you are gone: a neighbor you trust, a family member, or a pool company.

Safety: chemicals and children

Doing your own pool care means keeping chlorine and acid at home. A 2019 CDC report found that pool chemical injuries lead to more than 4,500 emergency department visits a year. The CDC's rules are simple: never mix pool chemicals with each other, keep each one in its original labeled container, keep them dry and out of direct sun, and secure them away from children and animals. In a Yuma garage in July, heat is worth thinking about too; follow the storage temperature on each label.

Water clarity is a safety issue as well. According to the CDC, drowning is the leading cause of death for children ages 1 to 4. A cloudy or green pool hides the bottom, so a child in trouble may not be seen. Keep children away from any pool that is unfenced or uncovered, and never leave a child near water without an adult watching.

Someone noticing problems early

Equipment wears out whether or not anyone is watching. Angi puts the life of a pool pump at about 8 to 15 years, depending on size, type and upkeep. A rising reading on the filter gauge, a new noise from the pump, or a small drip at the equipment pad is often the first sign of trouble. If you check the pool yourself every week, you will probably catch these. If you are away or busy, a regular visitor may catch them instead, while the problem is still small. For repairs, ask which Arizona Registrar of Contractors license the company holds and look the number up on the ROC contractor search (new tab).

Middle-ground options to ask about

You do not have to choose all or nothing. Some households do the routine work themselves for most of the year and want help only at certain times. Options you can ask a company about include service during the hottest months only, visits every other week instead of weekly, or service just while you are out of town. Not every company offers each of these, so ask, and get the terms in writing. A one-time pool cleaning can also reset a pool before you take it back over, and weekly pool service explains what a regular visit usually covers.
