What happens on the call

Yuma Pool Cleaning Pro is a free phone line, not a pool company. Its whole job is to get your call to a pool service company that works in your part of Yuma County. Here is the order things happen in.

  1. You call {{ site.business.phone_display }}. An automated assistant answers. It picks up at any hour, every day, so you can call after work, late at night or on a weekend.
  2. You describe the pool in a sentence or two. The assistant asks briefly what is going on. Plain words are fine: "the water turned cloudy after the storm" or "the pump is making a loud grinding sound."
  3. The call is transferred. You are put through to an independent pool service company that is owned by local people in the Yuma area. Before it picks up, the company hears a short summary of what you said, so you do not have to repeat everything.
  4. If the company cannot answer, your name, callback number, area and pool problem are passed to it so it can phone you back.
  5. From there you deal with the company. It looks at the pool, tells you what it thinks is wrong, gives you its own price, does the work if you say yes, and bills you directly.

What the line does, and what it never does

The line is a connection and nothing more. Knowing where its role ends helps you know who to ask about what.

The line does

  • Answer your call at any hour with an automated assistant
  • Ask briefly what the pool is doing and where you are
  • Transfer you to an independent local pool service company
  • Pass your details along for a call back if the company is busy
  • Explain on this website how to check a company before you hire it

The line does not

  • Clean pools, test water or handle chemicals
  • Repair pumps, filters, heaters or anything else
  • Give quotes, set prices or book appointments
  • Check licenses, insurance or reviews for you
  • Charge you anything, ever

The pool companies may pay the line a referral fee for calls. That fee never shows up on your bill from the line, because there is no bill from the line.

What to have ready before you call

The call goes faster, and the company starts with a clearer picture, if you know three things.

  • Where the pool is. Yuma, Fortuna Foothills, Somerton, San Luis, Wellton or Gadsden. Your community helps the call reach a company that works there.
  • What the pool is doing. Green or cloudy water, a pump that hums but does not run, a leak at the equipment, crust on the tile, a heater that will not light. If it started after a storm or after months away, say so.
  • The pool type, if you know it. Plaster or pebble, chlorine or saltwater, a heater or not. If you are not sure, that is fine. The company will see it in person.

If the pool sat unused while you were away for the summer, mention how long. A company plans a green pool cleanup differently from a weekly visit.

What to ask the pool company

The company is independent, so you decide whether to hire it. These questions help you compare one company with another and avoid surprises on the bill.

  • What is your Arizona ROC license number? The Arizona Registrar of Contractors licenses residential pool service and repair under the R-6 classification. Search the number on the ROC contractor search to see whether the license is current and what work it covers.
  • Can I have the quote in writing? A written quote should say what work is included and what it costs, so there is no confusion later.
  • What does a regular visit include? For weekly service, ask whether the visit covers brushing, skimming, baskets, water testing and filter care, and whether chemicals are part of the price.
  • Can you show proof of insurance? Arizona's ROC does not require contractors to carry liability insurance (workers' compensation aside), and insurance does not appear on the ROC search. Ask the company directly.
