What happens on the call

Nobody at Yuma Pool Cleaning Pro does pool work. The line is a free phone number with one job: getting your call to a pool service company serving your part of Yuma County. Here is the order things happen in.

  1. You call {{ site.business.phone_display }}. An automated assistant answers. It picks up at any hour, every day, so you can call after work, late at night or on a weekend.
  2. You describe the pool in a sentence or two. The assistant wants a short description of the problem. Plain words are fine: "the water turned cloudy after the storm" or "the pump is making a loud grinding sound."
  3. The call is transferred. You are put through to a pool service company in the Yuma area. It is independent of the line and locally owned. The company gets a brief summary of your problem first, which saves you telling the whole story twice.
  4. If the company cannot answer, your name, callback number, community and pool problem go to that company for a return call.
  5. From there you deal with the company. It inspects the pool, explains the likely cause, sets its own price, does the job if you approve, and sends its bill straight to you.

Where the line's job starts and stops

The line is a connection and nothing more. Knowing where its role ends helps you know who to ask about what.

The line does

  • Answer your call at any hour with an automated assistant
  • Ask what is wrong with the pool and which community you live in
  • Connect you with an independent pool business owned by local people
  • Hand your details on so a busy company can return the call
  • Explain on this website how to check a company before you hire it

The line does not

  • Clean pools, test water or handle chemicals
  • Repair pumps, filters, heaters or anything else
  • Give quotes, set prices or book appointments
  • Check licenses, insurance or reviews for you
  • Charge you anything, ever

A referral fee may be paid to the line by the pool businesses. You never pay the line anything, so that fee is never passed to you as a charge from the line.

What to have ready before you call

The call goes faster, and the company starts with a clearer picture, if you know three things.

  • Where the pool is. Yuma, Fortuna Foothills, Somerton, San Luis, Wellton or Gadsden. Your community helps the call reach a company that works there.
  • What the pool is doing. Water that is green or milky, a pump that buzzes but will not start, a leak at the equipment, crust on the tile, a heater that will not light. If it started after a storm or after months away, say so.
  • The pool type, if you know it. Plaster or pebble, chlorine or saltwater, a heater or not. If you are not sure, that is fine. The company will see it in person.

If the pool sat unused while you were away for the summer, mention how long. A company plans a green pool cleanup differently from a weekly visit.

What to ask the pool company

The company is independent, so you decide whether to hire it. These questions help you compare one company with another and avoid surprises on the bill.

  • What is your Arizona ROC license number? The R-6 license class, set by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors, is for home pool service and repair. Type the number into the ROC contractor search to check that the license is active and fits the job.
  • Can I have the quote in writing? A written quote should say what work is included and what it costs, so there is no confusion later.
  • What does a regular visit include? For weekly service, ask whether the visit covers brushing, skimming, baskets, water testing and filter care, and whether chemicals are part of the price.
  • Can you show proof of insurance? Liability insurance is not a condition of an Arizona ROC license, and the ROC search does not list it. The only way to know is to ask the company.
