Pool maintenance in Yuma is mostly a matter of rhythm. What does pool maintenance include?

A standing service plan is a repeating appointment, usually on the same weekday. The details differ by company, but the core tasks look much alike. The Pool and Hot Tub Alliance (PHTA), a trade group, lists water testing, brushing and vacuuming as weekly jobs, and HomeGuide's maintenance cost guide describes weekly visits that skim, brush, vacuum, clean the filter, top up the water and adjust the chemistry.

  • Leaves, bugs and dust netted off the surface
  • Skimmer and pump baskets emptied so water keeps moving
  • Walls, steps and floor brushed, then vacuumed
  • Water tested and adjusted, with the results noted
  • A glance at the filter pressure, the pump, the timer and the water level

That last item matters more than it looks. A careful pool technician who sees the same equipment every week tends to notice a new drip, a rising pressure gauge or a pump that sounds rough before it turns into a bigger repair.

How weekly pool maintenance in Yuma changes through the year

The same visit does different work in different months. Here is what drives the changes, using public weather data for Yuma.

  • Long hot seasonThe National Weather Service counts about 118 days a year at 100 degrees or more in Yuma (1991 to 2020 normals), with the first usually around April 25 and the last around October 6. Long, bright summer days mean the chlorine needs closer watching, so summer visits lean on testing.
  • Sunlight and stabilizerThe CDC lists sunlight as one of the things that lowers chlorine. Many outdoor pools use cyanuric acid, a stabilizer that shields chlorine from the sun; with it, the CDC advises keeping free chlorine at 2 ppm or more and pH between 7.0 and 7.8.
  • Monsoon and dustThe NWS monsoon season runs June 15 to September 30, and a NOAA weather memo names the Yuma area as a place where dust walls form, most often in late July and early August. After a storm, baskets fill and the filter works harder.
  • Mild wintersFreezing nights are rare; the NWS normal is about 0.1 days a year at or below 32 degrees. Winter visits are usually lighter, but the water still needs testing.
  • Power hoursOn current APS time-of-use plans, on-peak is 4 to 7 p.m. on weekdays. APS suggests running the pool pump off-peak, so ask the company to set the timer with your plan in mind.

Hard water is a year-round factor. The City of Yuma calls its Colorado River supply hard, at 18 to 22 grains per gallon. As pool water evaporates and gets topped up, minerals build, which is why many weekly plans include a quick brush of the waterline. Leaving Yuma for the summer: set up the pool before you go

Yuma is a winter home for many people. A 2017 to 2018 study for Visit Yuma and the Arizona Office of Tourism estimated about 71,000 winter visitors who stayed 30 days or more between December and May, and Yuma County says its population grows by about 90,000 in winter. Many of those homes have pools that sit through the hottest months with no one there to look at them.

That gap is when pools go wrong. The EPA says an uncovered pool can lose 12,000 to 31,000 gallons a year to evaporation, depending on where it is, and Yuma County Public Health calls green, unmaintained pools a major breeding source for mosquitoes that carry West Nile virus. A plan agreed before you leave keeps both problems small.

  • Keep the weekly visit going for every month you are away, not just the first few.
  • Give the company a gate code or key and tell neighbors who will be coming.
  • Agree in writing on a dollar limit for small repairs they may make without calling you, and how they reach you for anything larger.
  • Ask whether they send a note, readings or a photo after each visit.
  • Check that the gate still self-closes and latches. City of Yuma code calls for a fence or wall at least 5 feet high with self-closing, self-latching gates.
  • Ask about a pool cover. The EPA says a cover can prevent up to 95 percent of evaporation.

How to compare weekly pool service plans in Yuma

Two plans with similar monthly charges can cover very different work. These questions make the difference visible.

  1. What is on the written task list? Ask for each task and how often it is done, including the filter.
  2. How are chemicals billed? Included or charged by use, and what happens to the bill in the hottest months.
  3. Will you get visit reports? Some companies send readings or photos; ask if they offer it.
  4. How are repairs handled? Ask whether every repair is quoted first and which Arizona ROC license the company holds.
  5. What happens when you are away? Gate access, a repair limit and who they call.
