Each pool cleaning task, and why it matters in Yuma

Skimming

A leaf net lifts off floating debris before it sinks. Dust, palm fronds and drowned insects that reach the floor start to stain plaster and feed algae.

Skimmer and pump baskets

These two baskets catch what the net misses. When they pack full, water flow drops, the filter sees less water and the chlorine spreads less evenly.

Brushing

Walls, steps, corners and the tile line get scrubbed so algae cannot settle in. The PHTA says weekly brushing helps prevent algae and stains.

Vacuuming

Settled dirt is pulled off the floor. After a dust storm, fine silt can coat the bottom in a thin gray layer that a filter alone will not catch.

Filter care

Sand and some other filters are backwashed; cartridge filters are taken out and rinsed. A careful pool technician follows the filter maker's directions and watches the pressure gauge.

Water testing

A cleaning visit should finish with a test, because scrubbed walls in unbalanced water soon look dirty again. The CDC lists pH 7.0 to 7.8 as the target range.

Why the waterline gets extra attention here

The City of Yuma rates its Colorado River water as hard, at 18 to 22 grains per gallon. The city says calcium and magnesium cause most of it. When pool water evaporates, those minerals stay in the pool, and the white crust usually shows up first in the band of tile where the water level rises and falls. Brushing that band during regular cleaning keeps a soft film from hardening into scale. Thick scale that is already set is a separate job, covered on the pool tile and calcium cleaning page.

One-time and deep pool cleaning in Yuma

Some cleaning is not part of a routine. A single, thorough cleaning tends to make sense in three situations.

After a dust storm

A NOAA weather memo names the Yuma area as a place where dust storms form, most often in late July and early August. One storm can leave silt on the floor, a full basket and a filter that needs attention all at once.

A pool that sat empty of care

Many owners spend summers away. A pool that went months without brushing or testing may need a full scrub and vacuum, and if the water turned green, a green pool cleanup first.

Before selling or moving in

A clean pool shows better to buyers, and a new owner learns what condition the plaster, tile and equipment are really in once the water is clear.

For a sense of price, HomeGuide's 2026 guide lists a one-time or specific cleaning at about $200 to $400 nationally, depending on pool size and type of cleaning. That is a published national figure, not a quote from this line, and real quotes vary. The pool maintenance cost guide has more ranges.

Robotic cleaner or manual vacuum

A robotic cleaner runs on its own power and filter bag, crawls the floor and often the walls, and can keep a pool tidier between visits. It does not replace the rest of the cleaning: it will not empty the pump basket, clean the main filter, brush the tile line or test the water. A manual vacuum, run by a person through the skimmer, reaches corners and steps a robot may miss and lets the cleaner see the whole pool up close. Many pools use both.

Where backwash and drain water can go

Cleaning a filter or lowering the water sends pool water somewhere. The rules depend on whether the home is inside City of Yuma limits.

  • City of YumaThe city's stormwater page wants pool water sent into the sanitary sewer by way of the private cleanout on your own property, never a public manhole. Stop adding chlorine 48 hours before draining, drain slowly in low-use hours, and do not let water reach the street. City code needs the Director of Utilities' approval for pool drainage to the sewer, so call City of Yuma Utilities at 928-373-4500 first.
  • Unincorporated Yuma CountyFor places such as the Foothills and Gadsden, the county's pool drainage guide asks that water going anywhere other than the sanitary sewer have chlorine below 1 ppm and be clear and free of debris. It bars any permanent link to a storm drain or wash, and saltwater pools may drain to the sanitary sewer only.
  • Other townsIn Somerton, San Luis or Wellton, ask the town office before draining.

The city's guidance is written for draining a pool. For backwash water, ask City of Yuma Utilities and the cleaning company how they handle it.

A word on safety. Children should stay well back from any pool that has no fence or cover, and a grown-up should watch them the whole time they are near the water. Murky or green water hides the floor, which makes a swimmer in trouble hard to spot. According to the CDC, toddlers and young children from 1 to 4 years old die from drowning more often than from anything else. The CDC also says to keep different pool chemicals apart and never combine them; store each in its own labeled container, closed and out of reach of children and pets.
