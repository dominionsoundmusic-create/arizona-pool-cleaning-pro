The Hottest Pool Market in Arizona

Yuma averages 175 days above 100 degrees — more than Phoenix, more than Tucson, more than anywhere else in the state. Summer highs regularly push past 115 and occasionally touch 120. Water temperatures climb past 95, which more than doubles chlorine demand compared to spring levels. A pool that tests fine on Monday morning can test zero chlorine by Wednesday afternoon.

The heat is only part of it. Yuma sits in an active agricultural region, and wind-blown sand, dust, and crop debris load pools daily. The Colorado River water that fills most Yuma pools runs hard — calcium deposits on tile, heater cores, and salt cells just like it does everywhere in Arizona, but the extreme evaporation rate concentrates it faster here than in any other market.

All of that means a Yuma pool needs more chemical volume, more frequent debris removal, and more equipment attention than a pool in a milder climate. Weekly service is the baseline, not a luxury.

What Every Weekly Visit Covers

Same checklist, every week. Skim the surface, empty baskets, brush walls and tile, vacuum the floor. Test chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, and calcium hardness — adjust what needs adjusting. Check filter pressure, inspect the pump, and look at every piece of visible equipment. If something is off, you hear about it the same day.

Chemicals are included in the monthly price. Chlorine, acid, stabilizer, and anything else the pool needs on a routine basis. One number on your bill, nothing extra. In Yuma, where chemical consumption is higher than anywhere else in the state because of the heat, the included-chemicals model saves you from guessing what acid and chlorine will cost this month.

Where We Work

Central Yuma

The established neighborhoods around downtown, the Yuma Regional Medical Center, and the Arizona Western College corridor. Older homes with pools built in the 1980s and 1990s — aging equipment that benefits most from weekly inspection.

Foothills and Fortuna Foothills

The newer developments east of Yuma along the I-8 corridor. Master-planned communities with larger pools, many with salt systems and spas. The Foothills have grown rapidly and most pools here are in the 10-to-20-year age range where original equipment starts needing attention.

Somerton and San Luis

The communities south of Yuma toward the border. Agricultural areas with heavier dust and sand loading — pools here need more frequent basket emptying and filter attention than pools in the city center.

Repairs

When something breaks, we diagnose and fix it on site. Pumps, motors, filters, heaters, salt cells, lights, and plumbing leaks. In Yuma's extreme heat, equipment pads without shade push motor temperatures even higher — we inspect for heat stress on every visit and recommend shade structures when the pad layout allows it.
