Why Yuma Pools Need More Attention

Yuma is not just hot — it is the hottest city in Arizona and one of the hottest in the United States. The pool season never ends. Water stays warm enough to swim in every month of the year, and from May through October, water temperatures regularly push past 90 to 95 degrees. At those temperatures, chlorine burns off roughly twice as fast as it does at 80 degrees. A pool that tested fine at the start of the week can be zero chlorine and growing algae by mid-week.

Add the agricultural dust. Yuma sits in the middle of one of the most productive farming regions in the country, and wind carries fine silt and crop debris into pools daily. That organic material feeds algae, clogs filters, and settles into plaster if it is not brushed and vacuumed regularly. A pool in Portland might go two weeks between cleanings. A pool in Yuma cannot go one.

What Every Weekly Visit Covers

Same checklist, every week. Skim the surface, empty the skimmer and pump baskets, brush walls and tile, vacuum the floor. Test free chlorine, pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, and calcium hardness — adjust whatever needs adjusting. Check the filter pressure gauge, inspect the pump, and look at every piece of visible equipment.

In Yuma, the basket emptying and brushing steps take longer than in other markets because of the dust and debris load. The chemistry step uses more product because chlorine consumption is higher. That is why chemicals are included — you should not have to pay more because the weather was worse this week.

Chemicals Are Included

Chlorine tablets, liquid chlorine, muriatic acid, stabilizer, and anything else the pool needs on a routine basis are part of the monthly price. One flat number every month. In Yuma, where a pool can consume 30 to 50 percent more chlorine than the same pool in Phoenix, the included-chemicals model matters more than anywhere else.
